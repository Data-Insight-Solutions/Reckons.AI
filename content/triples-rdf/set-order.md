# Order belongs to the membership, not to the member

Some sets are sequences -- the steps of a recipe, the stages of a process. When order matters, the position is recorded against the PAIR of set-and-member rather than against the member, because the same thing can be third in one sequence and first in another. A step is not inherently step four; it is step four of this particular process.

## Why it is this way

**Note**

Most sets are not sequences and pay nothing for this. Ordering a set costs about four times as much storage per member as leaving it unordered, so it is worth applying only where the sequence actually carries meaning.

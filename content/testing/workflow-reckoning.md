# Ask for a reckoning on a space

A reckoning is the question you put to what you have already confirmed. You say where you are and where you want to get to, check what it will draw on, and a proposal comes back that you weigh yourself. It is the page the Learn section calls a reckoning, run start to finish on the Getting started space.

### What is built, and what is not claimed

The proposal card says how many confirmed facts the reckoning is based on, and the reckoning is given only your confirmed facts. This workflow does not show a proposal listing, option by option, the exact facts it used: the page describes that as the intent, and the card today shows the count only.

## How these screenshots were made

**What they show.** Every step runs on the **Getting started** sample, in which two people, Alex and Jordan, plan a camping weekend. Everything in it is invented.

**What is scripted.** The test uses no language model. The reckoning in the last step is a fixed reply written in advance to read like a real one, so the pictures show what each screen looks like and that it responds. They do not show how good a real model's proposal would be, which is measured separately.

## The steps

### 1. Open the Reckon tab on a space with facts

Open the Reckon tab while you are working in a space that holds confirmed facts. The page asks for a situation first. A space with no facts shows an invitation to add a source instead, because the proposal draws only on facts you have confirmed.

Where: the Reckon tab (`/reckoning`).

**What the test checks.** On the Getting started space the Reckon tab shows the Situation box and no invitation to add a source first.

### 2. Describe the situation

Type where you are now and what the context is. Next stays disabled until there are at least ten characters, so a one-word answer cannot be sent.

Where: the Reckon tab (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning/02-situation.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 2, Describe the situation. **What the test checks.** Next is disabled for an empty situation and becomes enabled once the situation is typed in.

### 3. Say what you want to reach

Press Next, then describe the outcome you are trying to reach. Situation and target are the two halves of the question: where you are, and where you want to be.

Where: the Reckon tab (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning/03-target.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 3, Say what you want to reach. **What the test checks.** Pressing Next reveals the Target box, and Next is enabled once the target is typed in.

### 4. Check what the reckoning will use

Press Next again. The Confirm card repeats your situation and target and says how many facts are in your space. The proposal draws only on those confirmed facts. You can go back and edit, or press the button to reckon.

Where: the Reckon tab (`/reckoning`).

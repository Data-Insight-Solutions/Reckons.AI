# Compare a new note against your space before keeping it

New claims never go straight into your space. After a note is read, Compare lays its claims next to what you already hold, labels each one, and lets you keep all of them, only the new ones, or none. It is the page the Features section describes as Compare, run on the Getting started space.

### What is built, and what is not claimed

This workflow compares a note's incoming claims against the facts already in one space. Putting two whole spaces side by side, and the model-written summary of the differences, are not shown: the summary needs a language model and this test uses none.

## How these screenshots were made

**What they show.** Every step runs on the **Getting started** sample, in which two people, Alex and Jordan, plan a camping weekend. The note typed in step 1 is made up.

**What is scripted.** The test uses no language model. A stand-in reader returns placeholder claims for the note, so the labels in the pictures show how Compare sorts claims, not how good a real reader's claims are. All three placeholder claims come out as new, so no picture shows a duplicate, reinforcing or conflicting claim; those kinds are exercised by the diff engine's own unit tests.

## The steps

### 1. Add a new note

On the Add tab, give a made-up note a title and a few sentences and press Extract facts. The claims it proposes are held as pending, outside your space, and the page opens Compare for them.

Where: the Add tab (`/ingest`).

**What the test checks.** Pressing Extract facts leaves the Add tab and opens Compare.

### 2. See the new claims against your space

The page is headed incoming vs. graph and names the note. It draws the new claims and the facts they touch, and counts the claims by kind: new, duplicate, reinforcing, conflicting or refining.

Where: the Compare page (`/compare`).

**What the test checks.** The Compare page names the note and shows at least one count.

### 3. Read each claim's label

Each incoming claim is listed with its kind. A conflicting or refining claim also shows the value your space already holds, so you can see what it would change before deciding.

Where: the Compare page (`/compare`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/compare/03-read-labels.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 3, Read each claim's label. **What the test checks.** Every pending claim is listed with a kind and its own accept and reject buttons.

### 4. Keep only what you choose

Accept all, accept only the new claims, drop the duplicates, or reject all. You can also decide one claim at a time. The test presses accept all.

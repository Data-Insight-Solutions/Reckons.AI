# Reckon with chosen statements, then accept one option

A Reckoning helps you decide something using what your knowledge base already holds. Here the decision is how to unblock the data pipeline that the Quick-Start example's project depends on. You choose the statements that describe the situation, you choose what you are trying to get (an open question your knowledge base has, or an asset to make), and Reckons.AI writes two options into your knowledge base as proposals. Nothing in them counts until you accept one. Accepting is one confirmation: its statements become confirmed, any jobs it proposes are queued in a single approval, and the other option is marked superseded. If an option needs something it does not know yet, it is held as an open question; answering it finishes the proposal.

The steps are a story inside the Quick-Start example itself, which the walk starts with open. A browser test plays that story on every change.

## How these screenshots were made

**What they show.** The Quick-Start example, opened as its own space, with one open question added by the test: "What does Data Pipeline v2 still need?" An example file cannot carry an open question yet, so the test writes that one row; everything else is the example's own content.

**What is scripted.** The test uses no language model: the proposal is a fixed reply written in advance (two options about the vendor contract, each with a statement, a picture job and a missing-input question). The job queue is a stand-in too: the test counts the request written for the job and never runs a model. So the pictures show what each screen does and that the standings change as described. They do not show how good a real model's options are.

**What is not shown.** Running the queued picture job, and the weather-style context blocks a later phase adds to the situation.

## The steps

### 1. Select statements on the canvas and reckon with them

Open the canvas and select Project Phoenix. A Reckon with these button appears in its panel. Press it: the Reckoning page opens with the statements that touch Project Phoenix already chosen.

Where: the canvas (`/`).

A pink box labelled &quot;Select statements on the canvas and reckon with them&quot; marks the Reckon with these button, shown once something on the canvas is selected." loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>The pink box marks the Reckon with these button, shown once something on the canvas is selected.</figcaption></figure>

**What the test checks.** With one entity selected the canvas offers Reckon with these, and pressing it opens the Reckoning page with at least one statement already chosen.

### 2. Choose the situation from the list

On the Reckoning page the statements you hold are listed with a checkbox each. Untick any the canvas chose, tick the three that describe the decision, and write a sentence about it: "We need to decide how to unblock the Phoenix data pipeline." The count says how many are chosen. Press Continue: the situation is saved as a Reckoning of its own, so reloading the page shows the same choice.

Where: the Reckoning page (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/02-choose-situation.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 2, Choose the situation from the list. A pink box labelled &quot;Choose the situation from the list&quot; marks the list of statements with their checkboxes and the count of how many are chosen." loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>The pink box marks the list of statements with their checkboxes and the count of how many are chosen.</figcaption></figure>

**What the test checks.** Three statements are chosen, the count reads 3 statements chosen, and after Continue the address carries the Reckoning's id.

### 3. Choose the target: a question to answer

Choose Answer a question. The questions you still have open are listed; pick "What does Data Pipeline v2 still need?". The target is kept as a link to that statement, not as a copy of its words. Choosing an asset to make is the other option, and the page then shows the assets you have.

Where: the Reckoning page (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/03-choose-target.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 3, Choose the target: a question to answer. A pink box labelled &quot;Open question&quot; marks the open question chosen as the target, among the open questions you hold." loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>The pink box marks the open question chosen as the target, among the open questions you hold.</figcaption></figure>

**What the test checks.** The open question is listed and can be chosen; the review step then reads Answer this open question, and the choice is still there after a reload.

### 4. Generate a proposal that is written into your knowledge base

Press Review, then Generate proposal. Two options appear. Each is already in your knowledge base as proposals: a dashed statement saying what it would add, a proposed job that has not been queued, and a hollow question for the input it still needs. The page says so: nothing is confirmed and no job has been queued.

Where: the Reckoning page (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/04-generate-proposal.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 4, Generate a proposal that is written into your knowledge base. A pink box labelled &quot;The two options&quot; marks the two option cards, each listing its statement, its job and its open question." loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>The pink box marks the two option cards, each listing its statement, its job and its open question.</figcaption></figure>

**What the test checks.** Two option cards appear, every row the proposal wrote is pending, no job is queued, and each option has one statement, one job and one open question.

### 5. Look at an option on the canvas

On an option's card press Show on the canvas. The canvas opens on that option alone, its proposed statements drawn dashed so they cannot be mistaken for what you hold. Use the control that stops showing one option to come back.

Where: the canvas (`/`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/05-show-on-canvas.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 5, Look at an option on the canvas. **What the test checks.** The address carries the option's focus, and the control that stops showing one proposal option is on the page.

### 6. Accept one option

Back on the Reckoning page press Accept on the first option. Before anything changes, a confirm step says how many statements will be confirmed, that one job will be queued in one approval, that the other option's rows will be marked superseded, and that one missing-input question stays open. Press the confirm button. The card says Accepted by you.

Where: the Reckoning page (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/06-accept-option.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 6, Accept one option. A pink box labelled &quot;Accept one option&quot; marks the confirm step under the option card, which says what accepting will change." loading="lazy" decoding="async" /><figcaption>The pink box marks the confirm step under the option card, which says what accepting will change.</figcaption></figure>

**What the test checks.** The confirm step names the statement count, one job in one approval, the superseded rows and the open question; after it the accepted option's statement is confirmed, exactly one job request exists, and every row of the other option is superseded.

### 7. Answer the missing input

The accepted option still has its open question. Type "vendor-quote.pdf" in its box and press Answer. The target of this Reckoning was that question, so answering it finishes the proposal and its standing reads Completed. No second job was queued.

Where: the Reckoning page (`/reckoning`).

<figure class="workflow-shot"><img src="/workflows/reckoning-v029/07-answer-missing-input.webp" width="1280" height="800" alt="Screenshot of step 7, Answer the missing input. **What the test checks.** After Answer the proposal's standing reads Completed and the number of queued job requests is unchanged.

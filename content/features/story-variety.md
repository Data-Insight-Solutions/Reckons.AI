# Every example story shows something different

> **Functional** — built and working, with rough edges still being smoothed.

The example collections that come with the app each have a guided story. Left alone, they drift into telling the same tour with the same controls. A script now reads every story in every example collection and lists what each one uses: which filters (for example, show only the facts you have confirmed, or only the links that jump to another collection), which layouts (hub, timeline, force, hierarchy and others), which entities it highlights, which buttons it offers, which jumps to other collections and which media. It fails when a story uses no filter at all, or when two stories use exactly the same group. Each story was then given its own mix, chosen for what its collection is good at, and where a story's wording claimed more than the filter really did, the wording was corrected rather than the check relaxed. A SPECIFIC STORY: the Data Insight Solutions example's story now starts from its link, without a click on the notice that used to ask you to start it (the link ends in &story=...&autoplay=1), uses its own mix of filters and six layouts, and walks to the contact sheet of frames from the example 3D scan of a ring, reached through a link that selects that entity. That story is generated from a script, so a change to it is a change to the generator, never a hand edit.

<p class="derived">This is built and working.</p>

## Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

THE CHECK COUNTS FEATURES, NOT QUALITY. It proves two stories differ in what they use; it cannot tell you the tour is good. In the last run it covered 11 stories in 14 files and passed (2026-11-25). For the ring step in the Data Insight Solutions story, the test checks that the right entity is selected, not how it looks on screen; a person has not yet watched that step. A story shows only what is built.

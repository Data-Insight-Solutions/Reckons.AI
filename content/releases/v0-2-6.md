# Reckons.AI v0.2.6

This release has one goal and a second one that came along with it.

The goal is the **evidence chain**. A fact in your space now points to the exact words it came from,
and that link survives saving, copying and syncing. Facts and their sources used to live in separate
places; this release joins them, because a decision is only as good as the evidence you can follow
back from it.

The second goal is **spaces you can stand inside**: generated pictures, 3D captures and whole rooms
drawn behind your graph. It was built in the same two weeks and is part of the same promotion, so it
ships here. It is less finished than the evidence chain, and the last section says where.

## The evidence chain

- **Open in source.** From a fact's evidence, Open in source opens the source, scrolls to the
  passage, and marks the words the fact was taken from.
- **A fact remembers where its words are.** When Reckons.AI reads a source and proposes a fact, the
  fact records the exact stretch of text it came from. That record survives export, import and sync.
- **A changed source says so.** If a source's text changes after a fact was taken from it, the fact's
  evidence is marked stale ("the source changed" or "the passage moved"). It is never quietly pointed
  at different words.
- **A space file carries its passages.** The passages behind your facts are written into the space's
  own file, after the facts. Copy that one file to another computer and the evidence comes with it.
  Sync, Google Drive backups, auto-save and export all include them.
- **The groundwork for decisions.** A proposal is now defined: two to four options, each citing the
  facts it rests on, and through them the passages. Nothing in the app makes or shows a proposal yet.

## Getting started arrives reviewed

- **Sample sources.** The Getting started space now comes with seven short sample sources (each says
  it is a sample) and 48 facts taken from them. Each fact shows its passage, who accepted it and when,
  including two corrections and two rejections, so the review history is real rather than blank.
- **Three facts left for you.** The Review tab opens with three facts waiting for your decision.
- **Review without a download.** Opening Review no longer asks you to download a model.
- **Shelly asks first, and only when needed.** Shelly asks for permission to use a model when you
  send a message, not when the tour opens.
- **Honest counts on Review.** Items handed to an agent read "waiting for an agent", and the page no
  longer claims anything was settled without you.
- **Collections.** A group of things you made is now called a collection everywhere, and groups made
  before this release are converted when the space opens.
- **View settings.** The panel at the top left of the graph is now View settings, and it holds the
  space's background controls.
- **A quieter graph.** The node that stands for the space itself is shown only at the "all" detail
  level.

## Spaces you can stand inside

- **3D captures on a node.** A node can carry a Gaussian splat, a 3D capture drawn as many soft
  points. You see it in the 3D graph and can turn it in the viewer. Splats hide the nodes, lines and
  labels behind them.
- **A background for a space.** A splat can sit behind the graph, as a backdrop or all around you.
  Choose its orientation, size, position and opacity, or level its floor, from View settings.
- **Import a scan.** Bring in a 3D capture from your device (.ply, .spz or .sog). Large files are
  stored on your device, never copied into the space's text.
- **Place things in the room.** Put a node's splat where you want it inside the background, at the
  size you want.
- **Three.js scenes.** Attach an example from threejs.org to a node, or use one as the background.
  It runs in a sealed frame and only loads when you ask it to.
- **Two example spaces.** *Generated assets* shows a prompt becoming a picture, a short video and a
  splat, with the license of every tool that made them. *Your room* walks through scanning a room,
  importing it and placing things inside. Prompt, Image, Video, 3D model, Gaussian splat and License
  are now built-in types.
- **Make your own, on your own machine.** If you have a capable graphics card, optional commands
  turn a prompt into a picture, video and splat, or a phone scan of a room into a splat. Every model
  and tool they use is MIT or Apache-2.0 licensed, runs locally, and is checked by a separate test
  before its result is used. In the Generated assets space, Shelly can ask that local service for a
  picture and attach what it actually made.

## Currents deliver again

Currents, which collect items from the web through your own n8n server, reach the app again. Import
the Currents Monitor workflow into your n8n server and connect it in Integrations; new items then
arrive as facts waiting for your review, and your earlier decisions are kept when an item arrives
twice. Without a connected n8n server a current delivers nothing, and its settings now say so
instead of claiming it is delivering.

## Safety and correctness

- **Models pinned to an exact version.** The models Reckons.AI ships with are fixed to one exact
  version, so a change can name the model that made it. Two are not: the Kokoro voice (its loader
  ignores the version) and any model you type into Settings yourself. You will download each pinned
  model once more, at its pinned version.
- **Dependency advisories cleared,** including in the build tools.
- **A more honest README.** It now says plainly which features send data off your device.
- **Older copies of Your room load again** after its room file was renamed.

## For developers

- **CI as a graph.** It says which checks can stop a merge to main and which only advise.
- **Who made a change, and why.** Agent commits carry `Model:` and `Intent:` lines, checked in CI;
  a pull request into dev may not carry commits that exist only on main.
- **One review queue** for the app, the MCP server and the local jobs (there were two).
- **Terminology checks that read only words people see.** The UI terms audit finds engineering words
  on screen by rule, and the term ratchet now counts prose, not code identifiers.
- **Steadier local models.** One batch size per device, and a retry when the model server restarts.
- **Visual review.** Each test workflow has a page with a diagram and a screenshot per step; each
  release gets a review space of its own; a local vision model can walk a workflow and report what it
  sees.
- **A generation bench** that says when a newer open model should replace the one in use.
- **A reckoning simulator core.** A scene read from the graph, stepped by a physics engine and
  checked against conditions. It runs from the command line only; nothing in the app uses it yet.

## Planned, not built

These are written into the roadmap, so you can read what is intended. None of them works yet.

- **reckons.space**, a registry of shared spaces where every listing names who shared it.
- **Simulated reckonings**: proposals tried in a physical scene, scored and replayed.
- **A room from a sentence**: a space background made from a text prompt.
- **A Create chapter in Getting started**, and stepping into a linked space by zooming into it.
- **An automated review for dev**, so agents check and fix every change before a person reviews a
  release.

## Not true yet

- **Proposals are a definition only.** Building options with their citations, and following a
  citation to its passage, move to 0.2.7. They had not started when this release closed.
- **Evidence points at text only.** A fact can point to a passage of text, not to a page, a box on a
  page, or a region of an image; that part of the anchor is not built.
- **Original files stay beside the space.** The passages travel in the space's file; the original
  documents they came from are still kept next to it, not inside its folder. Moving them is planned
  for 0.2.7.
- **Splats are 3D only.** The 2D view does not draw them. The way splats hide what is behind them was
  measured only in software rendering, not on a real graphics card.
- **Your room is a guided example,** not a finished feature. Its room is a reconstruction of a
  computer-made scene, not a real scan.
- **A crashing Three.js scene is not fully contained.** The sealed frame stops a scene from reaching
  your data, but a scene that crashes the browser's graphics driver can still take the page down.
- **Making your own assets is demanding.** It was measured on one Linux machine with one NVIDIA RTX
  3090. Picture, video and splat took about 15 to 55 seconds each there; a room scan took about
  20 minutes.
- **Currents were not tested against a live n8n server.** In the review before this release, an RSS
  current configured without one delivered nothing. An RSS feed cannot be read directly yet, only
  through n8n, and ranking is not connected.
- **One of this release's parts has a visual test.** The Review 0.2.6 space lists the rest as having
  none.
- **Some safety checks only advise.** The safety attestation and the dependency audits report, but
  cannot stop a merge to main.
- **Big spaces are still slow to draw.** This release did not work on it, and it was not measured
  again.

# Using reckoning to decide something

> **Functional** — built and working, with rough edges still being smoothed.

You have a graph and a decision. Reckoning reads the graph as SITUATION (where things stand), TARGET (where you want to be) and PROPOSAL (what would close the gap) — grounded in the facts you confirmed, not in what a model recalls about the subject.

## At a glance

**Audience**

Anyone at the point where research has to become a decision.

**Starts With**

A graph with enough confirmed facts to reason over, and a target you can state.

**Ends With**

A proposal you can trace back to the specific facts it rests on.

## Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

It is only as good as what you confirmed. Reckoning reasons over the graph, so a graph full of unreviewed pending facts produces a confident proposal resting on claims nobody checked. Run it after a review pass, not instead of one. It states which facts it used, so a proposal you distrust can be traced rather than argued with.

## Steps

### Say where you actually are

The situation is drawn from the graph, but you frame which part of it matters. A reckoning over an entire graph answers a question nobody asked.

### State a target you could tell you had reached

A target that cannot be checked produces a proposal that cannot be evaluated. 'Choose a platform by Friday' works; 'improve our tooling' does not.

### Read the proposal against its evidence

Follow the facts the proposal cites back into the graph. If it leans on something you never confirmed, that is not a bug in the reckoning — it is the graph telling you what to go and check.

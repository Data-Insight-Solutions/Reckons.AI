## At a glance

**Audience**

Collaborators, reviewers, a team checking its own consensus, or a learner checking their understanding against an expert's graph.

**Starts With**

Your graph, and someone else's exported graph file.

**Ends With**

A list of genuine disagreements, separated from mere differences of wording.

## Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

The hard part is telling A Real disagreement from A NAMING ONE, and that is not fully solved. Two graphs can say the same thing in different words and look like a conflict, or use one word for two things and look like agreement. Predicate matching by embedding was benchmarked across five models and does not separate synonyms reliably (16.7% caught, negative separation), so wording differences are surfaced for a human rather than resolved automatically.

## Steps

### Bring the other graph in as its own graph

Import their export as a SEPARATE graph rather than merging it into yours. Merging first destroys the very thing you wanted to see: whose graph said what.

#### Detail

**Page**

/kb

### Read the summary before the rows

The diff summary says what KIND of difference you are looking at — mostly additions, mostly conflicts, mostly rewording — so you know whether you are catching up on their research or arguing with it.

#### Detail

**Page**

/compare

### Take what you agree with, keep the rest visible

Accepting their fact does not erase yours. A conflict that stays recorded as a conflict is more useful than a resolution nobody can audit later — especially when the two of you turn out to have been reading different sources.

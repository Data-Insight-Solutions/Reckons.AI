# The thesis

Most knowledge tools store what you know. Reckons.AI stores HOW YOU KNOW IT — and refuses to fake it when it cannot.

## The tenets

### An unverifiable claim, made by the party it benefits, is not evidence.

> **What we believe** — a commitment, not a control. Nothing enforces this.

This is the spine. We arrived at it three separate times from three different directions: a stated purpose does not unlock a content gate, a weak similarity does not justify a link, and a source you control yourself is not a source. Each time the answer was the same — so we stopped treating it as a rule and started treating it as the thesis.

### We would rather show you no citation than a forged one.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

Every extracted fact quotes the sentence it came from — and we CHECK that the quote is really in your source. Language models paraphrase, and a paraphrased citation is a forgery that looks right. If we cannot find the quote, we drop it and lower our confidence. A missing receipt is honest. A convincing fake is not.

### The most useful thing a graph can hold is a well-formed absence.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

You cannot ask a question you have not discovered you have. So a fact whose object is still unknown is not a gap in the graph — it is the point of it. Subject known, predicate known, object still open, and four things stalled behind it. That turns 'go find out what we are missing' into 'answer this one question'. The knowledge you need to decide is usually a few people away; the hard part was never reaching them, it was knowing what to ask.

### A forced link is worse than an orphan.

> **What we believe** — a commitment, not a control. Nothing enforces this.

Disconnected facts are merely less useful. An invented edge is a lie the graph tells itself — it LOOKS like knowledge and is not. So if a new fact does not genuinely connect to what you already know, we connect it to nothing and say so. 'Is related to' is a non-link wearing the costume of a link.

### Export is a right. Distribution is a privilege.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

Your graph is a plain text file on your machine, and we will never withhold it from you — whatever it contains. But when WE are the one carrying your content to someone else, we are a participant in that delivery, and we take responsibility for what we carry. We gate only what we carry.

### A private graph makes no claim on anybody.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

Think privately. Be wrong privately. Change your mind privately. Your own graph needs no permission, no account, and no provenance — because it makes no claim on anyone else. Provenance is a claim made TO SOMEONE; alone with your notes, there is nobody to substantiate it to, and no reason to pretend otherwise.

### We will not describe a control we do not have.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

Our safety documentation states plainly which protections exist and which do not, and an automated check verifies them against the live code every week — recording the failures too. We have already caught ourselves overclaiming, and corrected it in public. A safety promise you cannot verify is just marketing.

### No account until an account is unavoidable.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

There is no sign-up, no email, and no server holding your graph — the app is static files and a browser database, so there is nothing to log in TO. Where a feature genuinely cannot work without an account, it is YOUR account with a service you chose: your API key, your n8n, your Cloudflare. Publishing and cross-device sync are where this will be tested, and neither is solved yet.

### Open source wins the tie.

> **Enforced in code** — there is a mechanism, and it runs.

The application is MIT licensed and your graph is stored in Turtle, a W3C standard rather than a private format with an exporter bolted on. If we disappeared tomorrow, your graph and the code that reads it would both still work. The honest part: the strongest models this app can call are not open, and a local one is measurably worse at extraction today.

### Knowing something should not require permission.

> **What we believe** — a commitment, not a control. Nothing enforces this.

Enclosing knowledge behind a login, a subscription, or a format one vendor controls is a choice somebody made, not a property of the knowledge. The tools for thinking with what you know should be free to use, read, and leave. This is a BELIEF, not a control — nothing in the code enforces it, and it is why the other tenets are shaped the way they are.

# It is a notes app, and the extra step is the point

The comparison with Palantir and Databricks is true, and it is not the product. Reckons.AI is a personal assistant that takes notes. You capture something by typing it, pasting it, or saying it out loud, and it works out the claims inside and asks you to confirm them. Everything else here, from the open standards to the 3D graph to the agent tooling, is what that extra step buys. None of it is the reason to open the app.

<p class="derived">It has 2 parts below.</p>

## Why it is this way

**Principle**

Saying this is not a notes app with extra steps was the wrong denial, and it stood on the docs welcome page until 2026-09-16. It is note-taking with extra steps, and the extra step is the product. Defending it by rejecting the category told a first-time reader that the thing they came for was the thing we refused to be.

**Honest Note**

This is the failure mode the product sits closest to, and it has been observed on real readers twice, on 2026-09-06 and again on 2026-09-08. Both times a careful first-time reader saw generic note-taking and storyboarding. The answer is not to argue harder that it is something else. It is to make capture effortless and let the difference show itself on the way out.

## In this section

### Accessibility is the feature, not a compliance chore

If the cheapest way to get a thought into the graph is to say it out loud, then the graph is open to people who do not type comfortably, do not sit at a desk, or have both hands full. Speech-to-text runs on the device, so the easiest way in is also the most private one. That is the argument for democratisation reduced to a single interaction: an ontology you must be trained to edit is not available to most people even when it costs nothing.

#### Why it is this way

**Principle**

Every barrier to capture is a barrier to entry, and they compound in the same direction. Needing an account, needing a keyboard, and needing to know what a predicate is are all the same kind of obstacle. Removing the invoice while leaving the vocabulary lesson in place would democratise nothing.

**Honest Note**

This is stated as a goal, not as a finished control. Whisper speech-to-text and Kokoro speech output both ship and are in production. A measured accessibility audit of the capture path, covering screen readers, keyboard-only use and touch target sizes across the mobile work, has not been done, so this is a commitment rather than an attestation.

### Teaching the shift from documents to things

Nobody arrives thinking in entities and relationships, and asking them to is how a tool stays confined to researchers. The Sources view is where that is taught. It takes a document you already understand and shows what it became: the things named inside it, and how they connect. The shift in perspective is demonstrated on your own material rather than explained in a tutorial.

#### Why it is this way

**Decided**

Matt, 2026-09-16: note capture belongs inside the existing Add flow, as an enhancement of the note page rather than a separate top-level tab. A second place to write a note is a second thing to learn and a second thing to keep in step.

**Principle**

The perspective shift is the onboarding. Someone who has watched their own paragraph turn into four things and three relationships has learned the model, and no glossary achieves that.

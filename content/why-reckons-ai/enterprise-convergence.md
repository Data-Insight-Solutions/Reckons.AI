# Everybody arrived at the same answer

Between Palantir's Ontology, Microsoft's Fabric IQ, Databricks' OntoBricks and the long-standing triplestores from Stardog and Ontotext, the industry has settled on one shape of answer and mostly rebranded it as an ontology or a semantic layer. Strip the branding and the machinery underneath is the same: typed things, typed relationships between them, and a store you can reason over. That is an RDF graph, whatever the brochure calls it. ## Why it is this way

**Principle**

When five competing organisations independently build the same abstraction, the abstraction is probably right. Convergence is the evidence, and we are one of the things it converged on.

## In this section

### The shared parts list

What Reckons.AI genuinely has in common with the enterprise stack: RDF triples as the unit of knowledge, Turtle as the format on disk, OWL and RDFS for classes and properties, PROV-O for provenance, and MCP so a language model can query the graph as a tool. These are W3C standards and an open protocol rather than our inventions, which is exactly why a file written by one of these systems can be read by another.

#### Why it is this way

**Principle**

Portability is the point. A Turtle file is a W3C standard, so the graph you build here can be loaded into GraphDB, Stardog or any conformant triplestore. None of us own the format, and lock-in is a product decision rather than a technical necessity.

### Palantir Foundry and its Ontology

The product that made ontology a boardroom word. Foundry's Ontology layer models an organisation as objects, links and actions over its data, and everything above it, from dashboards to agents to automations, is written against that model rather than against raw tables.

#### Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

These are third-party reported figures rather than a published price list, because Palantir does not publish one. Treat them as an order of magnitude. The shape of the number is the argument, not its precision.

#### Detail

**Pricing Model**

Custom-negotiated enterprise contract, with no public rate card.

**Pricing Note**

Reported first contracts at mid-market and large enterprises land between roughly $500,000 and $2,000,000 a year. Pilots start near $200,000, and full enterprise or defence deployments exceed $10,000,000 a year. First-year implementation cost commonly matches or exceeds the subscription itself.

### OntoBricks, from Databricks Labs

The closest twin to what we do. It turns Databricks Unity Catalog tables into a materialized knowledge graph: design an ontology in OWL, map it to tables with R2RML, materialize the triples into a Delta triple store, reason over them with OWL 2 RL, SWRL and SHACL, then query through a generated GraphQL API. Like us, it exposes the whole thing to a language model over MCP.

#### Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

OntoBricks is genuinely good, and its privacy story is real: Databricks states that your data and your triples never leave your workspace. The catch is not surveillance but prerequisite, because you must already be a Databricks customer for any of it to exist. It also predates Reckons.AI by two months, created 2026-04-07 against our 2026-06-09.

#### Detail

**Copy Permitted**

No. Under kb:license-gate, a non-OSI source-available licence does not authorize copying into an MIT project. Ideas only.

**Created At**

2026-04-07

**Differs From**

OntoBricks starts from warehouse tables you already have and derives a graph from their structure. Reckons.AI starts from your sources, meaning notes, PDFs, web pages, repositories and speech, and builds the graph as you review each claim. Theirs maps structure that exists; ours creates structure that does not.

**License**

Databricks License, which is source-available rather than OSI open source. GitHub reports the licence as NOASSERTION.

**Pricing Model**

The app is free; the platform it requires is not. It deploys as a Databricks App and depends on Unity Catalog, a SQL Warehouse, Model Serving and Lakebase, all of which are consumption-billed.

**Repo Url**

https://github.com/databrickslabs/ontobricks

**Stars**

367

**Validates**

This is the strongest single piece of evidence for convergence. An independent team at a major data platform, with no knowledge of this project, chose RDF triples, OWL, SHACL and MCP. Those are the same four choices Reckons.AI made, and two teams reaching one architecture separately is evidence the architecture is right.

### The specialists: Stardog and Ontotext GraphDB

The mature RDF platforms, doing this properly since before it was fashionable. Both offer real SPARQL, real reasoning at scale and federation, which are things we do not have. Stardog sells a knowledge-graph-powered semantic layer under a commercial licence; Ontotext GraphDB, now part of Graphwise, is one of the longest-standing triplestores.

#### Why it is this way

**Honest Note**

The free edition is the clearest contrast on this page, and it is about permission rather than price. GraphDB Free costs nothing and is forbidden in anything commercial. Reckons.AI is MIT, so you may build a business on it, fork it, resell it, or never mention us. Free of charge and free to use are different things.

#### Detail

**Licence Restriction**

GraphDB has a genuinely free edition, capped at two concurrent queries, which from version 11.0 must be requested as a licence and installed by hand. Its licence cannot be used for a site or service operated for profit, or one that generates revenue directly or indirectly, including through advertising.

**Pricing Model**

Stardog uses custom enterprise pricing reached through a sales conversation, with no published rate. GraphDB Enterprise is licensed per CPU core per machine, each cluster node licensed separately, and concurrent queries are capped at the number of cores you licensed.

### What the enterprise platforms do that we do not

Said plainly, because the comparison is worthless without it. Reckons.AI has no SPARQL engine, so the standard query language for RDF is not implemented. Our reasoner is a small forward-chaining subset of RDFS and OWL covering subClassOf, subPropertyOf, sameAs and inverseOf, rather than the OWL 2 RL profile OntoBricks runs. Our SHACL shapes are advisory and enforced nowhere. We do not cluster, we do not scale to billions of statements, and we have no row-level governance or audit story.

#### Why it is this way

**Principle**

State the weakness in the sentence that makes the claim. A comparison that lists only our wins is an advert, and readers discount adverts automatically.

**Honest Note**

Reckons.AI is version 0.2.0 with a single maintainer. If your problem is fifty million rows in a warehouse under a compliance regime, buy the enterprise product, because it is better at that and this page is not going to pretend otherwise.
